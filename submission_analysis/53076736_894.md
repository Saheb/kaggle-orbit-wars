## Failure mode 1 — "Save and strike" opening (the decisive failure)

Analyzed via ep=77862833 (our score=792 vs opp=682, upset loss).

Step-by-step trace of the first 30 turns:
- Both players start with 1 planet, accumulating ships.
- **Opponent fires at step 3 with ~8 ships** to grab a neutral → 2 planets by step 9.
- We sit on 1 planet for steps 1-19, accumulating 29 ships.
- **We first fire at step 20** — a single 28-ship launch. Too late.
- By step 25: opponent has 3 planets, we have 2. Production gap compounds from there.

**Root cause:** The agent learned "save up, send big fleet" rather than "probe early with small fleets to grab neutrals fast." An 8-ship probe at step 3 that captures a neutral is worth 17 extra turns of double production — far more valuable than a 28-ship strike at step 20.

**Why training didn't fix this:** The terminal ±1 reward cannot distinguish between a planet captured at step 3 vs step 20. The compounding production value of early expansion is invisible to the agent.

## Fire collapse at step 128+ is a symptom, not a cause

Initial hypothesis: fire collapses specifically after step 128 (= 2× rollout window).

**Validated and rejected.** Per-window fire rate correlates perfectly with planet deficit:
- When we're -6 to -8 planets behind at step 65, fire drops to near-zero by step 129.
- When we're at parity or ahead at step 65 (wins), fire rate stays 0.39–0.83 through step 256.

The step-128 collapse is the agent correctly reading a losing board state and stopping to commit ships — a rational but self-fulfilling capitulation. Rollout=512 (Rev12) also went passive, confirming rollout length is not the fix.

## What winning looks like for this agent

In wins, 141208 fires 0.32–0.65 with 82–533 total launches. Planet counts grow or hold through step 150+. Wins happen when the opponent is *less* aggressive, not when we're maximally aggressive.

The agents beating us at 900–1027 score fire 0.30–0.87 with 400–1200 launches, avg ship sizes 10–40. They fire many small-medium fleets constantly rather than infrequent large strikes.

## Key training implications

1. **Early expansion timing is the decisive variable.** Firing a small fleet at step 3 to grab a neutral beats accumulating and firing at step 20 with 3× the ships.
2. **The terminal reward can't see compounding production value.** A shaped reward for early planet capture (or production differential) would give the agent gradient signal where it currently has none.
3. **"Save and strike" is a learned failure mode**, not just passivity. The agent fires, but too infrequently and too late in the opening.
4. **Rollout length is not the bottleneck.** Both rollout=64 (141208) and rollout=512 (Rev12) produce passive agents. The issue is reward signal, not credit assignment horizon.
